Shipping a browser extension that speaks every language

A practical walkthrough of on-page translation with large language models: how to find the right blocks, batch them efficiently, cache the results, and keep the original typography intact.

Why translate in context?

Sentence-by-sentence translation loses context. When a whole paragraph is sent at once, the model can resolve pronouns, keep terminology consistent, and choose an idiomatic register instead of a literal one.

The extractor walks the DOM once and treats every element without block-level children as a translation unit, so inline nodes such as links or <strong> markup never split a sentence apart.

Typography fidelity

This paragraph uses a monospace font at 19px in dark red — the translation below must copy exactly these properties.

Good translation is invisible: the reader notices the meaning, never the mechanism behind it.

What happens under load

  • Identical strings are translated once and fanned out to every copy on the page.
  • Paragraphs are packed into batches of roughly 1,200 characters to cut round trips.
  • Failed batches are halved and retried, so one bad paragraph never breaks the page.
